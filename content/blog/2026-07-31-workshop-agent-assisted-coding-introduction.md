---
title: "URSSI Summer Fellowship: Workshop on Navigating Successes and Failures in Agent-Assisted Code Development"
date: 2026-07-31
author: "Connor McClellan"
---


The sudden widespread availability and adoption of agentic coding assistants has researchers scrambling to keep up with an accelerated pace of research software deployment, but comparatively little effort has been made understanding the long-term impact these tools will have on developers and the software they write. Emphasis is placed primarily on the products these tools can produce, rather than the process the developer goes through to get to that product. Predictably, the perceived performance of these tools are quite varied case-by-case: we simultaneously hear anecdotes about one team resolving a decades-old unanswered question in numerical physics while others cite horror stories about an agent deleting months of work. A new workshop developed for the next URSSI School in Research Software Engineering will explore the answer to the question: *What difference in a developer's process separates a successful integration of AI/ML tools from a failure?*

### What will the workshop consist of?

In this workshop, participants will interactively explore what successes and failures in agentic coding look like. Participants will be placed in a set of scenarios where they are able to clearly see both the limitations of agentic coding and its potential benefits in a setting curated and supervised by experienced software developers.

The specific goal of the workshop is to provide attendees with an example-based, hands-on experience of integrating agentic coding into their skillset in a way that does not undermine or replace their critical thinking, but may improve their software-development workflow efficiency.

The project will be successful if participants report a higher level of confidence with

1. Identifying scenarios in which AI/ML agents are likely to obtain the correct answer without supervision and have a low risk for harm,
2. Identifying scenarios in which AI/ML agents should not be used, and
3. Identifying scenarios in which usage of restraints can successfully counter the limitations of coding agents.

### Who is this workshop for?

The workshop will aim to provide early-career scientists, software developers, and anyone else who develops or uses scientific research software with a more concrete sense of when agentic coding is likely to be most useful with the least number of failure modes. Senior developers who have successfully integrated agentic coding into their workflows have developed this sense through trial and error, but junior developers do not yet have the practical experiential knowledge that comes from years of navigating problems in research software without the availability of such agents. As such, they may be less likely to hone critical decision-making skills as they practice problem-solving with the help of a coding assistance agent. This workshop, and the online resources developed in conjunction, will serve as early intervention to help early-career researchers benefit from the wisdom of more experienced senior software engineers who have had more opportunities to hone their intuition for when agentic coding solves problems and when it is most likely to create them.

### What else will we learn from how the workshop goes?

Since the workshop materials will involve participants writing code to solve similar problems both with and without agentic coding assistance, a by-product of this interactive experience will be a small body of data that can be further analyzed to identify the impact of agentic coding on code quality, maintainability, and scientific value. This will then provide information about how the code that software developers produce changes based on agent usage, restraint usage, and how the availability of a senior software engineer’s input and supervision (or, simply, the availability of sensible guidelines on how to best utilize these tools) may enable more effective usage of coding agents. As an additional layer of granularity, one repetition of the scenario’s challenges may include an unsupervised agent coding without restraints to provide further information about the specific limitations of current models.

It is important for the metrics to not just evaluate the code, but also the developer’s experience. It is suspected that developers who offload problem-solving and critical thinking to an agent may retain less information about the solutions to the problems they solved and have less familiarity with the code they or their coding assistant wrote after-the-fact. Both an exit survey administered immediately after the workshop and a follow-up survey sent out to participants several weeks afterwards will collect information about the participants’ experience during the workshop: how did they feel that their depth of understanding of the problem was affected by the use of a code assistance agent? Did placing restraints on agents improve their understanding of what the agents could and could not do effectively? Did participants notice a difference in their understanding of a problem if they used the agent to generate the conceptual solution to the problem itself rather than only using the agent to refine and debug code?
